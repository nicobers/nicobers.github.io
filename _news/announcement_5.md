## *Enhancing searches for gravitational waves from the Crab pulsar in LIGO’s O3b with adaptive noise cancellation*

Abstract:

Mass quadrupole gravitational radiation from the Crab pulsar is emitted at $\approx 59.22 \, {\rm Hz}$, i.e.\ twice the pulsar's spin frequency. The emission overlaps spectrally with narrowband interference generated near $60\, {\rm Hz}$ by the North American mains electricity supply, which powers the Laser Interferometer Gravitational-wave Observatory (LIGO). Here we employ adaptive noise cancellation (ANC) based on a recursive least-squares algorithm to filter out the mains power interference from only LIGO data. The ANC filter is validated by searching for signal injections in real data collected by LIGO in the second half of its third observing run (O3b) using a semi-coherent algorithm based on a hidden Markov model (HMM) and a maximum-likelihood matched filter known as the ${\cal F}$-statistic. A software injection Crab-like signal with strain amplitude $h_0=5.6\times10^{-25}$ is recovered in LIGO-Livingston O3b data after performing ANC. A pilot search for the Crab pulsar itself is also performed in LIGO O3b data in the band from $58.5 \, {\rm Hz}$ to $60.5 \, {\rm Hz}$. The search returns $29$ above-threshold candidates in a 0.02 Hz band around the Crab emission frequency in LIGO-Livingston data at a $1\,\%$ false alarm probability, and $11$ candidates in the two-detector network (LIGO-Livingston and LIGO-Hanford) at a $5\, \%$ false alarm probability. The candidates are consistent with residual nongaussian artefacts. The HMM log likelihood of the primary spectral feature at $60\, {\rm Hz}$ produced by the mains power interference falls from $\approx6.0\times10^3$ before ANC to $\lesssim 2.5\times 10^3$ after ANC, which is consistent to one standard deviation with the background log likelihoods throughout the search band in the two-detector data. The pilot search is performed separately in LIGO-Livingston and two-detector data to illustrate how idiosyncratic features in the noise spectrum of each interferometer impact the effectiveness of ANC. Particularly, the two-detector search leads to a significantly boosted side lobe at $\approx59.5$ Hz due to the inclusion of LIGO-Hanford data.
